# Your household, in balance

Michalek Finance is a private place to keep your shared finances clear. Look for the blue dollar sign in your browser tabs. All amounts are Australian dollars. Dates and schedules use Brisbane time.

## Getting started
Create your first administrator with your name, email and the setup token supplied by the app owner. Create a passkey when your device prompts you. Setup completes only after that passkey is verified.
Open Accounts and add your everyday, savings, cash and credit accounts. Choose **Would you like to upload a statement to start?** to begin with a PDF, or enter your opening date and balance manually. Credit owed uses a negative opening balance.
Invite your wife through Settings → Users. Share the private invitation link directly. She creates her own passkey and can manage all household finances.

## Passkeys and signing in
Use Sign in with a passkey and follow your device’s fingerprint, face or screen-lock prompt.
Open your account menu to add and remove passkeys. Give them recognisable names. Register a different device or authenticator for your backup passkey. Removing your final passkey is blocked with a reminder to add a replacement first.
Log out ends your current server session. You can sign back in with any registered passkey.
If you lose every passkey, another administrator can reset your access and give you a replacement invitation. If the only administrator loses access, contact the app owner.

When reviewing the **local demo household**, choose **Continue as demo admin** to
explore the fictional records without a passkey. This opens a separate Demo Admin
account. You can edit accounts and try administrator screens in the demo. The
session lasts up to one hour; after logout or a demo restart, use the same button
again. If sign-in fails, the button stays available to retry. This option only
appears in the local demo; your ordinary household still uses passkeys.

## Overview
Available balance combines current bank and cash balances and subtracts outstanding credit owed.
Use **This month** for month-to-date totals or **This year** for 1 January through today in Brisbane. The year view shows income, expenses less refunds and **Net income** (income less spending). The month view shows **Budget remaining**. The calendar beside the title still selects a historical month; choosing a month exits the year view. Previous months include the full calendar month. Transfers do not count as spending or income. Available balance always stays current; history charts and upcoming bills keep their explicitly labelled periods.
Use **Whole household** beside the month to filter records labelled Household or a specific person. Both people can see all records. Changing the month or year view keeps your chosen person, and the selections stay in the URL when you reload.
Click a summary to see the accounts, transactions or budgets behind it. Money in/out and Net income keep the selected period and person; Budget remaining opens that month's plan. Attention shortcuts lead to uncategorised transactions, statements needing review and bills due in the next seven days.

**Household balance & outlook** joins six calendar months of recorded available balances to three months of projections. The solid line is recorded history; the dotted line starts at today's balance, with a Today marker separating them. Switch **Graph / Table** for exact daily values and their recorded/projected status. History starts from your account opening balances and imported activity; gaps before the first account are not invented. The balance chart and forecast cover the whole household, including when you filter spending to one person. Projections use scheduled income, bills and remaining budgets; unscheduled, unbudgeted spending is not included.

**Top spending categories** summarises the five largest categories in the selected month or year-to-date period; select one for its detailed Expenses view. The dashboard stays high level, with no recent transaction list.

**Past spending** shows the last six complete calendar months, their recorded total and monthly average. Refunds reduce spending; transfers and income are excluded. It follows your ownership filter. Click a month or **Explore expenses** to open the Expenses report with those dates and that person retained. Months without imported history may be incomplete.

## Accounts and balances

Accounts use institution logos and soft background tints, with clear balances and a smaller owner label. Cards fit across wide screens and stack on phones. Owner colours stay consistent with transaction lists and ownership selectors. Credit cards show **Amount owed** in red when their ledger balance is negative, or **Credit balance** when positive; combined balances still use the signed amount. Filter by owner or enable **Show archived** to include older accounts.

Choose **Bank or institution** when adding or editing an account. ANZ, BOQ, CommBank, HSBC, ING, Macquarie, NAB, Up and Westpac include logos and colours. Existing recognised institution names pick up these defaults automatically. For another institution, choose **Other / no institution** and enter its name, or leave it blank for cash.

Use the palette button on a card, or open **Edit account → Appearance**, to preview and change its colour, choose an icon, upload a logo or turn off **Subtle background tint**. Logos accept PNG, JPEG or WebP up to 2 MB and 4096 × 4096 pixels; they are resized automatically. **Remove uploaded logo** restores the chosen icon or institution logo. **Use institution defaults** clears the account overrides and restores the tint. Save changes to keep the appearance. A failed image or save keeps your existing logo and editable values; choose another image, correct the indicated field or save again.

For a new custom institution, tick **Save as a reusable institution** before saving the account. Its name, colour and logo/icon then appear in the institution picker for other accounts. Each account can override that branding independently. Choosing a different institution restores that institution’s defaults; review the preview before saving.

Open an account card to see its own page: Transactions, Balance & spending, Upcoming & forecast, and Statements. Edit account changes its details and archive status. **Import transactions** offers **PDF statement** or **CSV export** for that account. Each account card, account page and import screen shows **Last recorded transaction** (or **No transactions recorded**). This is the newest saved transaction date, including refunds and transfers; uploading older history does not move it backwards. Opening balances, schedules and unapproved imports do not count. Current balance is prominent; opening balance/date sit quietly above the ledger, and the forecast stays in its own tab. The account forecast includes accepted scheduled bills, income and transfers into or out of that account; household budget allowances remain on the household forecast.
Opening balances establish the starting point immediately before activity on the opening date; transactions on that date are included. A 31 December closing balance can be used as the 1 January opening balance, provided all activity from 1 January onwards is recorded. A later export alone cannot fill an earlier gap.

**Balance & spending** has the same Date range control and shows daily closing balances for the selected dates, the balance before that period, money in, money out, net change and closing balance. Money in/out includes transfers and refunds as cash movements. Use **View balance values** for the exact numbers. The category breakdown uses only this account’s expenses less refunds within the selected dates, excluding income and transfers. Its category trends and transaction drill-downs keep the same account and dates; uncategorised spending in another account is not included. Missing history affects the balance chart too.
Record a transfer when moving money between tracked accounts or paying a credit card. The app records both sides together.
Archive an unused account through its details. History and its remaining balance stay available; settle its balance before retiring it.

## Transactions
Add income, expenses or refunds. Enter a positive amount and let the selected type determine its direction.
Use the correct expense category for a refund so it reduces that category’s spending.
Choose the account, date, category and owner. New entries suggest the matching saved-rule category as you enter a description. Choosing a category yourself, including Uncategorised, takes precedence. Editing an existing transaction keeps its current category. Notes are optional.
Filter activity by description, date, account, category, type or owner. Account, category, type and owner dropdowns support multiple ticked choices. In the category dropdown, **Select all** ticks every category, including **Uncategorised**, and keeps the menu open so you can untick anything you want to exclude. **All categories** clears the category ticks and removes that filter. Open **Date range** for **This month**, **This year**, **All history**, or custom From/To dates, then **Apply dates**. The panel and calendars open beside their buttons; Escape or a click outside closes the panel without applying unfinished custom dates. Search, dates and ticked filters share one compact toolbar on wide screens. On phones, **Filters** opens the other controls and shows the number of active filter groups. Removable tags show the active selections even when the controls are closed. The × clears the filters. Filters and page are kept in the URL; returning with Back restores the list and scroll position. These filters are also available inside each account’s Transactions tab. **Uncategorised** includes income, expenses and refunds without a category; transfers are already classified and are excluded. To see transfers, use **All categories** with **Transfers** in the type filter. Changing a row to Transfer removes it from an Uncategorised view as soon as it saves. **Select all matching transactions** selects every matching row across all pages, not just the 50 shown. Changing a filter clears selection. Individual checkboxes and **Clear selection** refine or reset it.
Change a single category directly from its coloured bubble in the transaction list, including inside an account. The arrow sits inside the bubble; click it to choose another value. Choose **+ Create category** to add a category or subcategory and apply it immediately, or **Uncategorised** to clear it. New categories use the transaction’s compatible type. Income offers income categories; expenses and refunds offer expense categories. Transfers keep their linked classification. A save conflict refreshes the row so you can review the latest value and try again.

Change **Type** from its bubble in the same row. Income, expense and refund changes save immediately; expense is money out and income/refund are money in. A category is retained only when compatible with the new type. For **Transfer**, choose the other account and **Save type**. If an equal opposite payment exists there on the same date, choose it to link the two without duplicating it; otherwise the app creates the opposite entry. Your original transaction, notes, receipts and import details stay attached. If similar transactions remain in your current filters, saving automatically opens **Remember this transaction** with the match count. Review and confirm **Save rule & apply to N** to change those existing matches and remember the transfer for future imports. This also works with Expense, category or Uncategorised filters after the saved transfer leaves the list. Cancel keeps only the transfer you just saved. Changing a transfer back asks you to confirm unlinking: both payments are kept and the other entry becomes income or expense according to its direction. Scheduled payments cannot change type here.

Use **Remember** on the right of a transaction row (below its amount on mobile). First set its category or change its type to Transfer. The confirmation shows **Found N matching transactions in the current filters. Apply to all?** Check or edit the remembered text and match mode, expand the matching payments, then choose **Save rule & apply to N**. It includes every page within your current account, date/month, search, category, type and person filters. Other filtered-out payments are not included. The review shows how many rows will change and how many already agree; category changes also show how many existing categories will be replaced. Cancel leaves the matches and rules unchanged. A concurrent change stops the entire update and refreshes the preview for review.

For a transfer, the rule remembers the source account, money-in or money-out direction, and other account. Matching payments must use that source account and direction. The confirmation also shows existing payments to link and new counterpart entries to create in the other account; these balancing entries may be outside your display filters. If several payments could match, choose the correct one for each row, or explicitly create a separate counterpart. A saved payment can link only once. Scheduled payments or transfers to a different account must be reviewed separately. Amounts, dates, notes and receipts are preserved. Up to 1,000 transfer matches or 10,000 category matches can be confirmed at once; narrow your filters or text for larger sets.

The confirmation explains **Also saves a rule for future transactions**: saving both updates the confirmed existing matches and creates an enabled rule for future matching CSV/PDF imports. Your current date and display filters only limit which existing transactions are updated now. Transfer rules also require their saved account and direction. Future CSV/PDF previews show **Auto transfer · saved rule** and the selected other account. Review and approve imports as usual.

Rows agreeing with an applicable saved category or transfer rule have a pale green background and **Matches saved rule** label. This is recalculated after paging or reloading. Manage, pause, edit or delete saved rules in **Categories & rules → Rules**. Rules are stored in the app's database. The green label describes the saved result; it does not mean a rule previously categorised that transaction. Use **Apply saved rules** in the Rules tab to review and fill uncategorised matches across active accounts, or the table’s **Remember** action to review existing matches within your filters.

Search fields have an **×** button (or press Escape) to clear the text. Optional transaction date filters have their own clear buttons. **Clear filters** resets the list while keeping the current account when viewing an account.

Use **Bulk category → Apply category** for selected income, expenses and refunds (up to 10,000 at once). Transfers stay selected but retain their linked transfer classification and are excluded from category edits. For a mixed income/expense selection, filter by type to assign a compatible category; Uncategorised can clear either kind. A concurrent edit rejects the whole bulk change so you can refresh and review.
Category, person, account and type chips use consistent colours with text labels. Category colours follow your chosen colour; categories sharing a colour get distinct automatic accents.
Open a transaction to edit it, inspect receipts or delete it. Deleting a transfer removes both linked entries. Remove receipts before deleting a transaction.

Use **Delete selected** in the selection bar to remove multiple transactions in the current filters, including across pages. Review the confirmation count: linked transfer entries outside the selection or in other accounts are included and removed together. Deletion cannot be undone. At most 10,000 entries, including transfer counterparts, can be deleted at once. If any entry has an attached receipt or has changed since the confirmation opened, nothing is deleted. Remove receipts first, or refresh and review the selection before trying again.
Deleting from a transaction’s details also checks the entries and versions you confirmed. If the payment changed or became a linked transfer after you opened it, nothing is deleted; refresh and review it before confirming again.
If someone edits the same record while you are editing, refresh and review their changes before saving again.

## Credit cards

Add each card under Accounts with type **Credit card**. It has the same transaction, statement and forecast tools as a bank account, with debt represented as a negative balance. For example, $500 owed is −500.00; an overpayment is positive. The account page also shows the amount owed in plain language. Cards are already included in net worth: do not add the same debt again under Assets & Liabilities.

Purchases, interest and fees are expenses; refunds reduce expenses. Repayments are **Transfer / card repayment** from the paying bank account to the card. Both sides are created together and neither counts as income or spending. Both accounts must have an opening date on or before the repayment. If the paying account starts too late, the review names it and offers **Fix [account] opening balance** without losing your review edits. Check the balance before the earlier history rather than merely shifting a date. A later statement for the other account flags that entry as a possible duplicate: leave it unchecked. If the opposite payment already exists as ordinary income or spending, use **Link this payment** in the import conflict panel to turn it into the linked transfer without duplicating it.

Upload a card PDF from setup or the account page. The reader converts ordinary positive amounts owed into negative ledger balances, uses running balances where available, and recognises CR credits. Review the signs against the original because banks use different conventions. Positive card transactions are suggested as repayments unless the description indicates a refund; choose the paying account or change the type to Refund as appropriate. Recurring card purchases can be accepted as bills or subscriptions. The forecast includes these accepted charges; it does not assume a bank’s minimum payment or automatically pay the card. Check due dates and payment requirements on the statement.

## Importing PDF bank statements

The account banner shows its institution logo and tint. Progress distinguishes upload, reading and review; completed steps have checkmarks. The CSV importer uses the same treatment for upload, column mapping and approval.

Start from the statement option in Add account, or choose **Import transactions → PDF statement** from Transactions or an account. PDFs can contain multiple months; six-month statements are supported. Each upload can contain up to 20 MB, 500 pages and 10,000 transaction lines. Split larger statements into smaller PDFs. Password-protected files must be unlocked before uploading.

The local reader extracts all pages and uses text recognition for scans. If the first reading finds no transaction rows, it automatically scans the pages again, including PDFs with selectable headings above scanned tables. If AI returns no rows, the app first tries the local reader. A statement that remains unreadable shows Needs attention instead of an empty completed review. For unfamiliar layouts, **Use AI to understand this bank’s layout** sends extracted text to the model selected in Settings → AI. It requires configured AI access. Email addresses and long numeric references are masked, but merchant names and financial details are included. The provider is requested to use no-retention processing. Leave it unchecked to keep extraction local.

1. **Opening balance:** check the opening balance and all source pages. An extracted opening balance is tied to the source; if none is found, enter it from your statement. Opening balances can be applied only to accounts with no transactions and a zero starting balance. If an opening balance or account history already exists, the app keeps its opening date and amount and continues importing. This also applies when another person adds a transfer after you opened the review. The inferred opening date is the earliest extracted transaction date, immediately before that transaction. You can move the opening date earlier, but an extracted opening amount remains tied to the PDF. The opening date must include the earliest selected transaction, even when that date is before the printed statement period. Existing account history is preserved.
2. **Review transactions:** inspect the readable transaction cards and categories. Use **All**, **Needs attention**, **Ready**, **Reconciled** or **Excluded** to focus the review. Counts span every page. A persistent action bar keeps selected/attention counts and Continue or Import within reach on a phone. Uncategorised rows are highlighted for review but may still be imported; invalid dates and conflicts need correction or exclusion. Twenty rows appear per page; descriptions wrap in full and edits remain when paging. Spending initially belongs to the account owner. Select **+ Create category** from the category dropdown to create and select a category or subcategory without leaving the review. Suggestions use merchant rules, previous activity and common merchants; the optional AI reader also suggests categories. Mark refunds and transfers correctly. Possible duplicate payments are initially excluded; include a genuine repeat only after checking it. Source amounts and dates cannot be edited into different financial records during confirmation.
3. **Recurring suggestions:** choose each bill, subscription, income or transfer pattern you want to track. Nothing is accepted by default. Patterns tolerate changing payment references and occasional missing periods. Two well-spaced payments from a recognisable recurring provider can also appear as a possible pattern; check these carefully. Review the expected amount, frequency, next date and **Type**. Choose **Transfer** and the other tracked account: **Transfer from** for money received, or **Transfer to** for money paid out. Transfers have no spending category. Other types use their matching category. Variable payments use the median amount as a suggestion. Only selected patterns are saved, starting in the future.
4. **Confirm:** check the selected/excluded totals and explicitly accept the review. Opening balance, transactions and recurring items are saved together. A failed confirmation leaves them unchanged. Repeating confirmation of the same file cannot duplicate its entries.

The reader recognises printed year headings and dates such as **06-Nov**, including statements crossing New Year. A separate processing date does not replace the transaction date. Wrapped descriptions remain together.

HSBC Everyday Global statements can print a date once for several payments and cashback credits. The reader keeps that posting date for the following blank-date rows, including continuations on the next page, and joins wrapped descriptions. It checks each printed running balance and the final debit/credit totals and transaction counts. A dated HSBC opening balance keeps its printed date. These source checks also apply when AI reading is enabled; AI cannot remove verified HSBC rows or change their dates, amounts or debit/credit direction. Review cashback as a refund where appropriate, and identify transfers before importing. Use a separate AUD statement or CSV for each account if a portfolio PDF includes several accounts or currencies.

If an older HSBC review missed transactions, open it under the account’s Statements tab and choose **Read again**. Confirm replacing the review, then check the new transaction count against **Transaction Number** at the end of the PDF. This refresh is available before import. Already imported records are preserved.

Qantas Pay statements are supported for the AUD wallet in a **bank** account (the card is prepaid). The reader joins merchant names split around the dated row, follows amounts onto the next page, and selects the AUD opening/closing balances. It uses the AUD wallet amount for an overseas purchase, not the merchant's foreign-currency amount. Credit vouchers are suggested as refunds. Prepaid reloads are suggested as transfers: choose the funding account in **Review transactions**, or change the type if the money came from someone else. AI reading preserves these source-verified transactions and only suggests categories.

If a Qantas Pay exchange line has an amount without a matching dated transaction, **Source & reading notes** highlights it and identifies its page and line. The reader does not guess a date or merchant, and a closing-balance mismatch remains visible. Download the PDF and obtain a corrected statement or CSV for the missing entry before importing. Other currency wallet entries are left for review rather than counted as AUD. Use **Read again** to refresh an older unimported review with the new reader.

Uploading the same PDF again automatically restarts a failed or older empty reading using your new upload options. Completed imports and non-empty reviews are preserved. For a saved review you want to refresh, choose **Read again** beside the source download. Confirm to discard review edits and read the original PDF again with its original options. This is available only before import; it does not change posted transactions or account balances.

No PDF reader can guarantee every layout or scan. Open **Source & reading notes** for the original text and reading notes. Highlighted source lines, invalid dates or a closing-balance mismatch require attention. Compare the original PDF; do not accept an incomplete extraction. Re-export a clearer PDF, try the optional AI reader, or use the bank’s CSV export. For dates without a printed year, use **Fix statement dates** in the review. Apply a year to all invalid dates, or choose each row’s full date when a statement spans New Year. The printed day, month and amount remain protected.

Uploaded PDFs are private to signed-in household members. Find the original download and a pending review under the account’s Statements tab. Once the PDF upload finishes, processing continues in the background even if you navigate away or close the app. The Statements tab shows Queued, Reading PDF pages, Analysing transactions, Ready to review or Needs attention. Use View progress to reopen a pending job. Failed jobs keep their PDF and offer Try again; completed PDF reviews remain saved for later. No financial records change before you accept the review. An account is created before the optional statement upload, so cancelling setup leaves an empty account you can revisit.

## Resolving matching payments during import

**Transfer reconciliations** shows clear matches to transfers already recorded in both accounts, even when the banks use different descriptions. Choose **Accept reconciliations (N)** to accept every clear match across the upload. Expand **Review matched transfers** to inspect them first, accept one, or review a row individually. Accepted rows are green and labelled **Reconciliation accepted**, with an undo button. Approving the import attaches each source row to its existing entry; no extra payment is created and balances, descriptions, ownership, notes and receipts stay unchanged. The projected CSV balance excludes these already recorded movements.

A clear match requires the same account, date and signed amount, a complete transfer pair, one matching payment, one source row, and an entry not previously claimed by another import. Repeated amounts, broken pairs or already imported entries remain for individual review. If either transfer entry changes before approval, the import stops for review rather than applying a stale choice.

CSV and PDF reviews show matching payments at the top, including later pages. Each card names the source row, date, amount and matching account/payment. **Open matching payment** opens its details in another tab; **Review row** jumps to the exact row. **Skip row** excludes an already recorded payment. **Link this payment** confirms an ordinary, unscheduled opposite payment in the other account: on approval only the missing account entry is added, and the existing payment becomes a transfer with its amount, date, owner, notes and attachments preserved. Its category is cleared and it no longer counts as income/spending. **Undo link choice** clears the choice before approval. **Keep as a separate payment** requires confirmation and should be used only for genuinely separate payments; transfers add both account entries. A stale or reused link blocks the entire import and identifies the row to review. One saved payment can link to only one source row.

## Importing bank CSV files
Export your transactions as a CSV from your bank and add the account first. Open the account and choose **Upload CSV**, beside **Upload statement**. The account is already selected. You can also use **Import CSV** on Transactions and choose the account there.

1. **Upload:** choose a file up to 5 MB and 10,000 transaction rows. Comma, semicolon and tab-separated exports are supported. The app detects whether the first row contains column names. Check **First row contains column names** and change it if needed; headerless files use Column 1, Column 2 and so on, preserving the first transaction. Check the sample includes your first transaction. Files with headers need a unique, non-empty name for each column.
2. **Map columns:** date, description and amount columns are prefilled where possible without requiring AI. Check every suggestion against the sample. Choose a signed amount column, or clear it and choose separate positive debit and credit columns. Negative amounts normally mean money out. If your bank uses positive amounts for spending, select **Reverse amount signs**. Select the exact date format; ambiguous dates default to Australian day/month/year. A wrong format can change dates even when they look valid. If present, map **Running balance (optional)** to the balance after each transaction; headerless amount/balance columns can be suggested when their running totals reconcile.
3. **Review & approve:** choose **Review transactions**. Categories are filled automatically using your merchant rules, previous transactions and recognised merchants, just like PDF statements. Review or change these suggestions. Check the target account, dates, amounts and selected money-in/money-out totals. Change an amount or date through **Change mapping**, then preview again. Invalid rows are excluded; possible duplicates start excluded. Select a legitimate duplicate only after checking it. You can deselect any other row you do not want to import.

**Check your balance** shows the file’s date coverage, source/selected/excluded row counts and projected app balance at the end of the file. A verified running-balance column also supplies the bank’s opening and closing balances. A difference requires acknowledgement before approval; check missing earlier exports, excluded/duplicate rows and the account opening balance first. A bank export can contain only a recent portion of the history you requested. The app never invents missing transactions or silently changes the account’s opening balance.

Mark positive refunds with Refund and the original expense category. Mark transfers and credit-card repayments with Transfer, and select the other tracked account; both entries are created together. A later CSV from that other account offers clear matches as transfer reconciliations.
Edit descriptions, categories and owner labels as needed. **Approve & import** saves only the selected valid transactions. No transactions are saved during upload or mapping. **View account** returns to the updated activity. CSV reviews are temporary; if you leave before approving or the preview expires after 24 hours, upload the file again.

Approving the same file again will not duplicate its transactions. The approved mapping is remembered for the account and suggested for later compatible exports. A different layout receives fresh suggestions. CSV imports do not change your opening balance or create recurring schedules; check the account’s opening date and balance cover the imported history.

## Remembering categories during review

In either a CSV or PDF review, choose a category and tick **Remember category**. This immediately applies it throughout that upload, including other review pages, without pressing **Suggest categories**. The matching text and number of other updated transactions appear below the checkbox. Recognised trailing bank period/reference codes are left out: for example, `Direct Credit 396297 IVV PAYMENT OCT26/00823316` and `Direct Credit 396297 IVV PAYMENT JUL26/00823296` share the remembered text `direct credit 396297 ivv payment`. Other descriptions start with exact matching. Adjust **Remembered text** and **Match descriptions** to use either the whole description or text it contains. Matching ignores case and extra spaces; the text must still match the checked transaction. Expenses and refunds can share a category; income is matched separately and transfers are excluded. Inclusion checkboxes stay as you left them.

Automatically updated cards have a pale green background and an **Auto categorised · remembered in this upload** label. You can still change any row individually. Changing the category on the checked row updates matching rows again. Unticking the box restores the automatically changed categories while preserving individual edits made afterwards. Changing the checked row’s description or type, clearing its category, or excluding it cancels remembering.

The rule is saved together with your transactions when you approve the import. Cancelling a review saves no rule. Future imports show **Auto categorised · saved rule** on matched rows. Manage, pause or delete these rules in **Categories & rules → Rules** (mobile: **More → Categories**), or open **View transaction rules** from the review. Exact-description rules run before broader contains rules. Current saved rules are rechecked when reopening an unimported PDF review, correcting dates or changing a row’s type. Both reviews keep your explicit category choices (including Uncategorised), remembered choices, transfer choices and inclusion checkboxes. Rules do not silently rewrite already imported records; use **Apply saved rules** or the table’s **Remember** review.

## Optional AI suggestions
Open **Settings → AI** (More → Settings → AI on mobile). An administrator pastes the **OpenRouter API key**, chooses an **AI model** labelled with its maker and model name (for example **Anthropic · Claude Sonnet Latest**), then selects **Save AI settings**. Get a key using the OpenRouter link beside the field. The saved key is encrypted and never displayed again; leave the field blank to keep it, or paste a replacement. **Check key** checks whether OpenRouter accepts the entered or saved key without sending financial data or running the selected model. Model availability and credits are checked when you request suggestions. **Remove saved key** removes the key entered here; if the app owner configured a server key, that key is used again. OpenRouter charges usage to your OpenRouter account.
AI can suggest column mappings and categories. Redacted sample descriptions and CSV samples are sent to OpenRouter when you request suggestions.
Amounts and dates always come from your CSV. AI cannot commit an import or overwrite a chosen category.
Review every suggestion. If AI is unavailable, finish the import manually.

CSV category suggestions run in batches of 25 with visible progress. A failed batch is retried once; **Retry suggestions** resumes unfinished work while retaining completed suggestions and all source rows. Uncertain, unknown or incompatible category suggestions remain uncategorised; valid suggestions in the same batch are kept. You can review them manually and approve without AI. AI suggestions never remove transactions or change their dates and amounts. A category failure cannot itself explain a balance difference. If AI requests are temporarily limited, wait a minute before retrying; you can still approve your reviewed transactions.

## Categories and transaction rules

Choose an **Icon** and **Colour** when creating or editing a category. The live preview shows the result; **Automatic** uses a recognised category name or its parent icon. Saved icons and colours appear in transaction badges, selectors, budget rows and expense breakdowns. Over-budget rows also say **Over budget**, so colour is not the only indicator.
Open **Categories & rules** in the sidebar or the mobile More menu. Separate **Categories** and **Rules** tabs each have search. Categories can include archived entries; rules can be filtered to Enabled or Paused. Clear the search or filters to see the full list. Categories are separate from Settings. **Assign unique colours** gives every category a distinct saved colour, including archived categories and those outside the search. **Undo colours** restores the previous palette during that visit. A concurrent colour edit prevents the entire update or undo; refresh and review before trying again. Default income and expense categories are included. Add category creates a new one; choose Parent category to make a subcategory. Parents and children must share the same income/expense type, with one subcategory level.

Click a category to rename it, change its colour, move it under a parent, or archive it. Remove category deletes only an unused category without children. Used categories must be archived to preserve history. Archiving a parent also archives its children; restore each category you want to use again. Budgets and transaction filters currently use the exact selected category, so a parent and its subcategories have separate allowances.
In the **Rules** tab, add a description-matching rule such as Woolworths → Groceries, or choose **Transfer between accounts** and set its source account, direction and other account. **Contains** matches part of a description; **Exact description** matches the whole description. Both ignore case and extra spaces. Exact-description rules run first, followed by contains rules; lower priorities run first within each group. Open **Advanced** in the rule editor to adjust priority.
Your explicit category takes priority. Rules suggest categories during imports and new transaction entry, and apply to API-created transactions without an explicit category.

For existing uncategorised transactions, choose **Apply saved rules** in the Rules tab. The review lists every matching income, expense and refund across active accounts, including later pages, with its date, account, amount and proposed category. It uses all enabled category rules regardless of the Rules search/status filter. Confirm **Apply to N transactions** to fill those categories together. Previously chosen categories, archived accounts and transfers are preserved. Cancel makes no changes. If a transaction or rule changed during review, use **Refresh data**, review the new matches and apply again. To replace existing categories or apply transfer rules to existing records, use the transaction table’s **Remember** action and its confirmation.

## Monthly budgets
Plan → Budgets sets an allowance for each expense category. Spending includes all owners and subtracts refunds.
Choose a month with the custom month picker to review **Budget**, **Spent** and **Remaining** side by side. **Set budget** applies to that month only by default. Tick **Repeat monthly** and enter **Number of months** (1–120) to repeat the same allowance. The count includes the starting month; for example, November plus 3 months covers November, December and January. The form shows the exact period before saving.

Click a budget amount to **override just the selected month**, including the first month of a repeating plan. Other months keep their planned amount. Set zero to allow no spending for that month. **Restore plan** removes the override and returns to the planned amount. Saving a revised plan keeps existing monthly overrides; if the plan no longer covers an overridden month, **Remove override** clears that standalone amount. Click Spent to review transactions in that exact category and month.

Existing ongoing plans still repeat until changed. Where plans overlap, the most recently starting active plan wins; an older ongoing plan can resume after a temporary plan ends. To change a plan, use **Set budget** with the same category and starting month, then save the revised allowance and duration. Overview and Forecast use each month's effective allowance, including overrides.
Unspent amounts do not roll over. Over-budget categories stay visible so you can adjust your plan.

Calendar fields open a custom day picker with month/year navigation, highlighted selection and a **Today** shortcut. Optional schedule end dates have a clear button. Use arrow keys to move between visible dates or months, Enter to select and Escape to close without changing the value.

## Budget vs actual reporting

Open **Budget vs actual** in the sidebar (under **More** on mobile), or **View budget vs actual** from the budget list. Choose a month with the calendar control. The report covers the whole household, labels the current month as partial, and separates:

- **Budget:** the effective allowances for that month, including manual overrides.
- **Actual spending:** recorded expenses less refunds, excluding income and transfers.
- **Upcoming bills:** unmatched expense schedules due today or later in the selected month.
- **Available after bills:** budget minus actual spending minus upcoming bills. A negative value is a shortfall.

The six-month chart compares budget and actual spending. Switch to **Table** for exact amounts; choose a month in the table to open its category breakdown. Category rows show allowance, actual spending, under/over variance, upcoming bills and the amount left after bills. The compact **Used + bills** column shows actual spending plus upcoming bills on a fixed **0–150%** scale, with a **100%** marker and the percentage on the right. Green means within budget; red means over, including a shortfall from upcoming bills. Bars stop at 150%, but the label keeps the true percentage above that. Categories without a budget show a dash; spending against a zero-dollar budget shows **Over budget · no allowance** instead of an undefined percentage.

Filter **Over budget** for categories whose recorded spending already exceeds their allowance, or **Unbudgeted** for spending and bills with no allowance. Zero-dollar budgets are still budgets.

On desktop, faint vertical separators help you follow each column across category rows. Mobile shows the same figures in a stacked layout.

Click a category's **Actual** amount to see the underlying transactions, including refunds, for that exact category and period. **Edit budgets** opens the same month's budget plan. All report totals include unbudgeted categories; the budget editor's Spent/Remaining summaries cover only categories with a budget. Categories and subcategories remain separate. Upcoming bills are included within their category's allowance, not added on top. Completed months show recorded actuals without projecting missed bills into the past.

## Bills, income, transfers and subscriptions

In a schedule’s **Appearance** section, choose a custom icon and colour and check the preview before saving. **Use automatic appearance** restores its category/type defaults. The same identity appears in upcoming payments, recurring items and the Overview. Upcoming items show **Due** or **Renews** with the payment date, while retaining overdue/this-week/later groups.
Plan → Bills & income keeps your regular paydays, bills and transfers. **Upcoming** groups occurrences into **Overdue**, **This week** and **Later**. **Recurring items** lists the schedules for editing. Filter by owner, and switch between these views without losing that selection. Subscriptions use the same schedule system.
Set an amount, type, account, starting date and weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly or annual frequency. For transfers, choose **From account** and **To account**; other types use a category.
Monthly dates stay anchored to the original day. A schedule starting on the 31st uses the last available day in shorter months and returns to the 31st afterward.
Planned occurrences do not create transactions automatically.
Use **Record payment**, **Record income** or **Record transfer** on or after the occurrence date, or **Match transaction** to link an existing transaction with the same account, type and amount. Each occurrence can be matched once. Recording a transfer creates both linked entries; matching requires an existing complete transfer between those accounts. Transfers affect each account’s forecast in the correct direction and remain excluded from household income and spending. Transfers between cash or credit accounts leave available funds unchanged; transfers to assets or liabilities reduce available funds while updating the tracked value.
Cancelled schedules stop future projections; existing recorded transactions remain.
Subscription monthly and annual totals are normalised estimates.

## Cash-flow forecast
Plan → Forecast offers 30-, 90-, 180- and 365-day views.
It starts with available funds, adds scheduled income, subtracts scheduled bills and allows for remaining budgets.
Budget allowances subtract already recorded and scheduled category spending before being spread across the remaining days. This prevents counting bills twice.
For example, a $1,000 category budget with $200 already spent and a $300 upcoming bill leaves $500 for other spending. The forecast includes the $300 bill on its scheduled date and spreads the $500 allowance across the remaining days. If scheduled bills exceed the budget, they still count in full and the extra allowance is zero. Bills without a category budget also count. Monthly overrides and each plan's repeat period determine the allowance used for each month.
Assign recurring bills to the same exact category as the budget they should consume. A $200 Subscriptions budget with $150 of recurring bills in Subscriptions projects $200 in total, including $50 for other spending. Categories and subcategories have separate allowances; a bill in a different category does not consume this budget.
Transfers between cash or credit accounts, including credit-card repayments, do not change total available funds. Scheduled contributions to assets and repayments to separately tracked liabilities reduce available funds; withdrawals and new borrowing add to them. Unscheduled, unbudgeted future spending is not included.
Unrecorded occurrences due today are carried into the next day of the forecast. Older unrecorded occurrences need your review and are not projected automatically.
Use it as a planning estimate and review the assumptions shown beneath the chart.

## Net worth

Net worth is the main summary, with assets and liabilities shown separately beneath it on smaller screens. Asset classifications have distinct icons and soft colours; valuation dates remain visible. Bank accounts keep their institution identity in the composition list.
Tracked accounts are counted automatically.
Net Worth offers **1 month**, **3 months**, **1 year** and **All history**, with **Graph / Table** for plotted values. The breakdown groups cash/accounts, investments, property, other assets and debts, each with its subtotal. Positive account balances contribute to assets; negative bank or credit balances contribute to liabilities.

Use the separate **Assets & Liabilities** page to add dated valuations for property, investments, superannuation, vehicles or debts not already represented by an account. Reach it from the sidebar, Net Worth, or More on mobile. Assets and debts have separate groups and totals, with owner bubbles and each item's last valuation date. Open an item for its current value or amount owed and an equation showing the last valuation plus later transfers. This movement is not investment performance. The **Activity** tab shows its history graph and transactions; **Valuations** holds dated values and their edit/delete controls. **Update value** adds a dated valuation. The graph combines closing valuations with later transfers and excludes future entries from current value.
Update values as they change. Historical totals use the latest recorded closing valuation on or before each date, adjusted by later transfers. With no valuation yet, recorded transfers start from zero; history before your first valuation or transfer is not invented.
Edit or delete an incorrect valuation from the asset’s details.

## Private receipts
Open a transaction and choose Attach receipt. JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF files up to 20 MB are supported.
Receipts are stored privately in Cloudflare R2. Both signed-in household members can view and download them.
If storage has not been configured, the upload explains what is needed. Failed uploads do not leave a usable attachment record.
Confirm deletion to remove a receipt permanently.

The Settings landing page links to **People** (administrators only), **AI**, **Release notes** and **System details**. **All settings** returns to this directory. Settings → People → Users provides Add user, role changes, new invitations, access resets and deletion.

## Expense reports

Open **Expenses** to review spending by category, account or person. Use **Date range** for This month, This year, All history or custom dates, then refine accounts, people and categories in the same compact toolbar. Account, Person and Category dropdowns support multiple ticked options and stay open while you select. Choose the All/Everyone option to clear that filter. Selecting several values includes any of them within that dropdown; different dropdowns combine, so a payment must meet each active filter. Selecting both a parent category and its child does not count the child twice. **Search expenses** matches descriptions across the report, charts and drill-downs; clear it with **×** or Escape. Choose **Uncategorised** in Category to find spending without a category. A parent category filter normally includes its subcategories. **Select all** ticks every expense category, including archived categories and **Uncategorised**, and keeps the menu open. After using it, each tick is independent: untick a category or subcategory to exclude just that category, even if its parent stays ticked. Your selections survive reloading. **All categories** clears the ticks and restores normal parent/subcategory filtering. The category breakdown shows each actual category separately. The summary shows Net expenses and Spending. Refunds reduce net expenses, and transfers and income are excluded. Household spending stays under Household rather than being split between people. The report includes a stacked category chart and the matching transactions. Archived categories and accounts remain available for historical reporting.

Use **Month**, **Week** or **Day** above **Expenses over time** to group the bars. Weeks run Monday to Sunday; the first and last bar include only dates inside your selected range. Category colours stay consistent with the category labels. Refunds reduce that category’s spending in the period; a negative category total extends below zero. Quiet periods remain on the chart. Scroll horizontally for longer ranges, and use the **Graph / Table** tabs above it to switch to exact category and net totals. Arrow keys switch tabs when focused. The graph starts with the five named categories with the most activity, keeps Uncategorised separate, and combines the rest into Remaining categories. Spending and refunds within that group keep their separate positive and negative stacks. Use **Show all chart categories** to expand the compact legend and bars, or **Show top 5 categories** to simplify them again. The Table view always lists every category and its exact totals.

The filters sit above the summary. On wide screens, **Expenses over time** and **Expense breakdown** sit side by side, followed by the full-width transaction list. On smaller screens these sections stack. The breakdown starts with eight categories, accounts or people, with their amounts and percentage of positive net expenses. **Show all** expands the list in the page; **Show top 8** collapses it again. **Included transactions** shows the description and date, account, editable type and category, person, net expense and a separate Remember action, newest first. Refunds are labelled and shown as negative expenses. Use **Next** and **Previous** to browse ten transactions at a time; changing report filters returns to the first page. On narrow screens, each transaction’s details wrap below its description. Account names open the account’s ledger.

To review recurring items from a statement already imported, open its account → Statements → View. **Recurring items from this statement** offers patterns supported by the posted transactions, excluding providers already tracked. ## Correcting statement dates and recovering from errors

When a PDF omits the year, **Fix statement dates** appears above the review steps. Enter the year and choose **Apply year to invalid dates**. Category edits and review choices are kept. For a statement spanning New Year, expand **Review individual dates**, choose **Fix row**, and select the full date in that row. The printed day, month and amount are preserved. Corrections are saved to the review, so they survive reloading. Future or impossible dates remain excluded until corrected.

A date before an account’s opening date offers **Edit account** on the same screen. The final confirmation provides **Fix opening balance**, **Fix transfers** or **Fix recurring items** buttons when those sections need attention. Invalid rows stay excluded; valid rows can still be imported. If a value cannot be verified from the PDF, use **Check source PDF** and **Read again → Change reading options**, or leave that row excluded. Reading again replaces the unsaved review only after confirmation. It does not change previously imported transactions. You can change the year or retry without AI on the saved PDF.

Failed forms now keep a persistent message beside their controls. Correct the indicated fields and submit again. **Refresh data** checks current records without replaying the failed change. After a connection failure, refresh first to check whether the server already saved it. Open form entries are retained during refresh. A stale bulk-deletion confirmation closes after refresh so you can review and confirm the current selection again. A failed transaction deletion keeps the detail window open so receipts and other blocking details remain accessible.

**Retry PDF upload** reuses the selected file with the current reading options. Receipt uploads allow selecting the same file again. AI-settings links from import and error controls open in another tab, keeping the current file, options and review in place. File download failures stay beside the file link; choose it again to retry. List-loading failures provide **Try again**. Passkey management and user invitations keep their controls alongside persistent feedback. Expired or used invitations require a new link from a household administrator; unavailable server/storage services require the app owner to restore the service, then the on-screen retry can continue.

## Editing categories wherever transactions appear

The category bubble can be changed in the main Transactions list, account ledgers, Expenses, Expense drill-downs and transaction details. Choose **+ Create category** to add and immediately apply a category. Inline changes show **Saving…**, then **Saved**. A failed save keeps your chosen value, shows the currently saved value and offers **Retry save** beside the field. Charts, totals and filtered lists update after saving. A transaction can disappear from a category-filtered view when it no longer matches that filter.

Use the **Type** bubble in Expenses or its drill-downs to correct a payment. For a transfer, choose its other account and **Save type**. Cancelling keeps just the type change already saved.

Transactions, account ledgers and Expenses have a **Remember** button in each transaction’s actions area. ## Transfers to assets and liabilities

Assets and liabilities appear in transfer account selectors, labelled **Asset** or **Liability**. Choose them when recording a transfer, changing an existing payment’s type, reviewing an import, creating a transaction rule or scheduling a recurring transfer. You can also open an asset or liability and choose **Record transfer**; its destination is preselected.

A contribution increases an asset’s tracked value. A repayment reduces the amount owed on a liability. Withdrawals and new borrowing work in the opposite direction. Both linked entries are recorded; transfers remain excluded from income and spending. Asset and liability movements are included once in net worth and are excluded from available bank/cash balances.

Each manual valuation is the **closing value for its date**, including all transfers on or before that date. Only later transfers adjust it. A note in the transfer controls explains when an existing valuation already covers the payment. If that closing valuation did not include the payment, update the valuation. With no valuation yet, transfers start from zero. The asset’s Transactions section shows its movements, and Value history includes both valuations and movements. A negative amount owed represents credit. Import statements into the source bank or credit account, then mark the relevant payments as transfers to the asset or liability.
